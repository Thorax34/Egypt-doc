# Credits and fact-check log (prototype)

Illustrations are original simplified cartoons made with code. They are NOT historically exact
reconstructions (crowns, clothing, boats, temples and the map are symbolic).

## Claims in the prototype narration

| Claim | Status | Source consulted |
|---|---|---|
| Egypt grew along the Nile, in north-eastern Africa | Established | General knowledge; verify before publishing |
| The yearly flood left fertile silt; farmers grew wheat and barley | Established (simplified; emmer wheat is the typical wheat) | History on the Net, "Egyptian Farming" https://www.historyonthenet.com/egyptian-farming |
| Upper and Lower Egypt were united about 5,000 years ago | Broadly accepted c. 3100 BCE; the date range proposed by scholars is wide, and whether the Narmer Palette records a real event or is symbolic is debated | World History Encyclopedia, "Narmer" https://www.worldhistory.org/Narmer/ (notes dates from c. 3150 to c. 2680 BCE and doubts that one king did it all) |
| Kings built pyramids as royal tombs | Established (most widely accepted purpose of the Great Pyramid) | World History Encyclopedia, "The Great Pyramid of Giza" https://www.worldhistory.org/Great_Pyramid_of_Giza/ |
| Great Pyramid over 4,500 years old (shown as "around 2600 BCE") | Approximate; reign of Khufu given as c. 2589-2566 BCE by one source and construction c. 2600 BCE by others | Same page; Wikipedia, "Great Pyramid of Giza" https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Pyramid_of_Giza |
| Egypt changed over time; Old, Middle and New Kingdoms | Established; the prototype shows names only, with no dates | Verify against a scholarly overview before expanding |

## Honest limits of this research
- These were quick web searches of general-audience and encyclopedia-style pages, not primary
  scholarship. Before publishing, check each claim against books such as I. Shaw (ed.), The
  Oxford History of Ancient Egypt, and museum resources (e.g. British Museum, Metropolitan
  Museum of Art).
- The simplified map and crown colours (white = Upper Egypt, red = Lower Egypt) are conventional
  symbols, not evidence of what people wore.
- No quotations are used.
